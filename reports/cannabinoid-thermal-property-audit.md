## Evidence boundaries

- Lovestead and Bruno (2017) directly measured CBD and Δ9-THC vapor pressure
  over limited temperature ranges and calculated predicted normal boiling
  temperatures. Those data are not measurements for CBG, THCV, CBDV, or the
  acidic cannabinoids.
- Wang et al. (2016) measured acid-cannabinoid decarboxylation kinetics in
  vacuum-oven extract/reference conditions at specified temperatures. The
  rates and losses are not universal onset/completion temperatures for dry
  flower or a vaporizer load.
- García-Valverde et al. (2022) measured degradation in defined GC-injector
  and thermal-treatment conditions. Injector temperature and residence time
  are not device sample temperatures.
- Urvashi et al. (2024) measured acid conversion in a pressurized-liquid
  extraction cell. Those process temperatures are retained as process data,
  not generalized to dry-flower samples or device settings.

## Unresolved evidence gaps

1. No direct atmospheric boiling observation was located for these
   thermally labile cannabinoids; even the THC/CBD atmospheric values are
   extrapolations.
2. Compound-specific vapor-pressure curves remain unavailable for CBCA,
   CBDA, CBGA, THCA, CBG, CBDV, and THCV.
3. No general device-to-sample thermal-transfer or load-specific evaporation
   dataset supports a universal cannabinoid setpoint or recovery fraction.
4. Compound-specific thermal-stability, oxidation, and secondary-product data
   remain sparse for CBCA, CBGA, CBDV, and THCV; analogy-only products are
   marked as unresolved in the records.
