# Valencene

## Identity

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Preferred name | Valencene |
| IUPAC name | (1R,7β,8aα)-1,2,3,5,6,7,8,8a-octahydro-1,8a-dimethyl-7-(1-methylethenyl)naphthalene |
| CAS number | 4630-07-3 |
| PubChem CID | 9855795 |
| Chemical family | Bicyclic Sesquiterpene |
| Molecular formula | C15H24 |
| Molecular mass | 204.35 g/mol |
| Stereochemistry | Chiral; the natural citrus form is (+)-valencene [^1] |

## Aroma and sensory character

Sweet citrus, orange, grapefruit, and woody character.

## Occurrence outside cannabis

- Valencia orange (*Citrus sinensis*)
- Grapefruit (*Citrus paradisi*)
- Mandarin (*Citrus reticulata*)
- Parsley (*Petroselinum crispum*)

## Physical properties

| Property | Value |

| Data source | PubChem CID 9855795 (123 °C @ 11 mm Hg); Sigma-Aldrich (274 °C, lit.); NIST WebBook lists no phase-change data for CAS 4630-07-3 [^1] |

## Thermal-extraction context

{{include includes/boiling-point-vs-device-note.md}}

Valencene is a low-volatility sesquiterpene; atmospheric boiling has not been independently confirmed by NIST, and evaporation from botanical matrix occurs at far lower temperatures via vapor-pressure-driven loss.

## Reported biological activity

### Human evidence
No verified controlled human study of inhaled isolated valencene was identified for this archive [^2].

### Animal or laboratory evidence

{{include includes/preclinical-evidence-note.md}}

In vitro and animal studies report anti-allergic mast-cell effects, anti-inflammatory cytokine suppression (mouse, intraperitoneal), and antimicrobial activity at high concentrations; a skin-protection study is topical, not inhalation [^3].

### Traditional or anecdotal claims
Citrus aromatics used traditionally in perfumery and flavouring.

## Cannabis laboratory results

Trace in Italian hemp cultivars (≤0.4 g/100 g of hydrodistilled essential oil) [^4]; no batch-level peer-reviewed quantitative data were located for drug-type flower. Consult [Lab Results](../lab-results.md) for batch-level measurements.
