# D-Limonene

## Identity

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Preferred name | D-Limonene |
| IUPAC name | (4R)-1-methyl-4-(prop-1-en-2-yl)cyclohexene |
| CAS number | 5989-27-5 |
| PubChem CID | 440917 [^4] |
| Chemical family | Cyclic Monoterpene |
| Molecular formula | C10H16 |
| Molecular mass | 136.23 g/mol |
| Stereochemistry | The (4R)-(+)-enantiomer (D-limonene) is the citrus form; (S)-(−)-limonene (L-limonene, CAS 5989-54-8) is a distinct identity kept separate |

## Aroma and sensory character

Bright citrus, lemon, orange peel, and fresh terpene notes.

## Occurrence outside cannabis

- Orange peel (*Citrus sinensis*)
- Lemon rind (*Citrus limon*)
- Grapefruit (*Citrus paradisi*)
- Juniper (*Juniperus communis*)

{{include includes/boiling-point-vs-device-note.md}}

Pure limonene liquid exerts significant vapor pressure well below 176 °C.

## Reported biological activity

### Human evidence
A double-blind randomized trial found that vaporized d-limonene reduced the acute anxiogenic effects of inhaled Δ9-THC in healthy adults who intermittently use cannabis [^2]. A standalone mood-lifting or anxiolytic effect of d-limonene alone (independent of cannabis) is not established by that trial [^3].

### Animal or laboratory evidence

{{include includes/preclinical-evidence-note.md}}

A peer-reviewed review of limonene pharmacology summarizes gastroprotective, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activity in animal and cell models, predominantly via oral or injected dosing [^3].

### Traditional or anecdotal claims
Commonly associated with uplifting citrus aromatherapy traditions.

## Cannabis laboratory results

Among the most abundant monoterpenes in cannabis flower: measured at 525–2,644 µg/g dry weight across five Canadian cultivars (GC-MS) [^5], and the vaporized-limonene trial's source flower was analyzed for limonene content [^2]. Refer to batch COAs in [Lab Results](../lab-results.md) for detected limonene concentrations.
