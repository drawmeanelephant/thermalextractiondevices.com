## Regulatory Overview

The Netherlands operates a tolerance ("gedoog") policy under the Opium Act: sale of up to 5 g in licensed coffeeshops is not prosecuted, while the supply side was historically unregulated (the "backdoor problem"). The **Controlled Cannabis Supply Chain Experiment** (wietexperiment) launched April 7, 2025: ten municipalities participate, and licensed growers legally supply participating coffeeshops. One-year results (April 2026) were reported as largely successful, with fines issued for non-compliance.

## Regulatory History

- **1976**: Opium Act reform establishes the tolerance policy.
- **2003**: Office of Medicinal Cannabis (BMC) established for medical supply.
- **2020–2024**: Experiment legislation and grower selection (10 growers).
- **April 7, 2025**: Experiment operational in participating municipalities.
- **2026**: Experiment extended; results evaluated.

## Licensing Structure

- **Experiment growers**: up to ten licensed growers supply participating coffeeshops (quality-controlled production).
- **Medical producers**: licensed under BMC/GMP for medicinal cannabis.
- Coffeeshops remain locally licensed under tolerance rules.

## Laboratory / Quality Framework

- Experiment supply is quality-controlled (defined standards, laboratory analysis) — a move toward regulated quality for the non-medical chain.
- Medical cannabis is produced under GMP and tested per pharmaceutical standards.
- **Public data**: no public per-batch COA database located.
