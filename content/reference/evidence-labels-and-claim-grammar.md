# Evidence Labels and Claim Grammar

## Classification Tiers

To prevent folk pseudoscientific overgeneralization, all biological, pharmacological, and thermal claims in this archive must use standardized evidence labels:

| Tier Label | Criteria & Definition | Permitted Claim Verbs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observed in Humans** | Controlled double-blind clinical trial or observational human study | *Observed in clinical trials*, *Demonstrated in human subjects* |
| **Preclinical / Animal** | *In vivo* rodent or animal model assay | *Observed in animal models*, *Demonstrated in preclinical rodent assays* |
| **Cellular / In Vitro** | *In vitro* cell culture or enzyme assay | *Exhibited in cell culture*, *Inhibited enzyme activity in vitro* |
| **Traditional Use** | Ethnographic or historical herbal remedy documentation | *Documented in traditional herbal practice* |
| **Consumer Report** | Observational survey or self-reported user experience | *Reported by consumers in observational surveys* |
| **Marketing Claim** | Unverified commercial breeder or vendor marketing statement | *Claimed in producer promotional material* |

## Forbidden Verbs & Misleading Phrasing

- **Forbidden**: "Myrcene causes couch-lock", "Limonene cures anxiety", "Pinene prevents memory loss".
- **Permitted**: "Preclinical rodent studies observe sedative responses at elevated β-myrcene doses", "Double-blind aromatherapy trials observe reduced anxiety scores in human subjects inhaling lavender essential oil".
